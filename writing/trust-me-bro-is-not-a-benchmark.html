Trust me bro is not a benchmark.

Every AI demo looks smart until a real document shows up.

30 Oct 2026

If you have been in an AI demo, you know the phase. The model answers a question. The answer sounds clean. The room nods. Nobody asks for the eval. That is the trust me bro phase, and every AI demo has one.

The problem is that "sounds right" and "is right" are two different products. A model can write a confident answer about a contract it never read, a tax rule it half-remembered, or a number it made up. It does not blush. It does not say "I am not sure." It just keeps typing. Funny right??

For a long time I thought the fix was a better prompt. More context. "Please do not hallucinate." That is not a strategy. It is a prayer with extra steps. The model is the easy part. The system around it is the job.

The boring part is the product

So what does the system look like? The boring version: find the right documents, keep their structure, retrieve the evidence that matters, and make every answer point back to a source. If the answer cannot show you where it came from, it is not an answer. It is a vibe.

Then you need the part nobody puts in the demo: the eval. A small, hard set of cases built from real failures. The question that broke last week. The document that confused the parser. The edge case a customer found before you did. You run the set on every change. You look at the traces. You fix the failure, and the fix becomes a test. If you cannot replay a failure, you cannot fix it. You can only hope.

The part where it stops being a demo

I work on tax software now. That changes the stakes. A wrong answer is not a funny chatbot moment. Someone files a return. The reviewer needs to see the evidence, not just the conclusion. So the output is not a chat transcript. It is a case file with citations, permissions, and a human review step before anything goes out. The AI can draft. The system has to prove.

I am not anti-model. I am anti-handwave. I do not trust the model. I trust the checks around it: retrieval, citations, traces, regression tests, latency and cost budgets. The model gets the credit when it works. The harness gets the blame when it does not.

Every AI demo has a trust me bro phase. The difference between a demo and a system is whether you end it. Show me the eval. Show me the failure. Show me the fix. Then I will trust you. Not because you said so.

Trust me bro is not a benchmark. A demo is not an eval.
